# Problem Evidence

Figures for the submission's opening paragraph. **Nothing here is invented.** Each claim carries its source and a confidence tier, because a fabricated or over-stretched statistic is the fastest way to lose a technically-minded judge.

## Confidence tiers

- **A — Independent or professional-body source.** Safe to state plainly.
- **B — Industry report, methodology not fully public.** State with attribution.
- **C — Vendor marketing.** Commercially motivated. Use only as illustration, always attributed, never as the headline number.

---

## Tier A — safe to state plainly

**Industry service benchmark: maintenance requests are expected to be acknowledged within 24 hours and routine repairs resolved within roughly 48 hours**, faster for urgent items.
→ This is the deadline FieldRelay exists to protect. It is also the number that makes the problem concrete: the window is short, and it is consumed by waiting for humans to answer telephones.

**NARPM's Code of Ethics obliges members to "respond promptly to requests for repairs"** where the management agreement makes them responsible.
→ Useful framing: responsiveness is a *professional obligation*, not merely a service preference. That raises the stakes of a missed call from inconvenience to compliance.
Source: [NARPM Code of Ethics](https://www.narpm.org/code-of-ethics/)

## Tier B — state with attribution

**"One in three negative reviews stems from upkeep issues"** — 2024 Voice of the Resident Report.
→ Ties slow maintenance directly to reputation, and reputation to occupancy.

**Maintenance concerns are consistently among the top reasons tenants leave.**
→ Supports the cost argument without needing a precise dollar figure.

**Turnover economics:** a 150-unit community turning units in 12 days rather than 7 loses roughly **$48,000 in rental income per month** during peak season, before contractor coordination costs.
Source: [Revolution Rental Management](https://www.revolutionrentalmanagement.com/property-management-blog/reducing-tenant-turnover-how-fast-maintenance-responses-impact-rental-property-success)
→ Use carefully: it is a modelled figure for a specific community size, not a survey result. Attribute it and state the assumption.

## Tier C — vendor marketing, illustration only

These circulate widely and are almost certainly directionally right, but every source found is a company selling maintenance-automation software. **Do not lead with these.**

- "Property managers spend around 40% of their week on maintenance coordination"
- "20 hours per week on vendor coordination with manual tracking"
- "65% of property management calls arrive outside standard business hours"

Sources: [OxMaint](https://oxmaint.com/industries/property-management/property-manager-vendor-contractor-management-guide-2026), [Layer3Labs](https://www.layer3labs.io/guides/ai-answering-service-for-property-management), [Callin](https://callin.io/residential-property-management-answering-service/)

If one of these is used, phrase it as: *"Maintenance-automation vendors put the figure at roughly 40% of a manager's week — directionally consistent with what the 24-hour acknowledgement benchmark implies."*

## Recommended opening paragraph

Following the winner's structure — prevalence → cost → duration → closing window → failure:

> A maintenance request is supposed to be acknowledged within 24 hours and resolved within about 48. Almost none of that window is spent fixing anything. It is spent on the telephone: calling a plumber who does not pick up, leaving a message, waiting, calling the next one, then calling the tenant back with a time you are not sure of. One in three negative resident reviews traces to upkeep, and maintenance is consistently among the top reasons tenants leave — so the cost of the window closing is not a complaint, it is a vacancy.
>
> **We asked: what if the calls just got made — and the only thing a manager had to do was say yes or no?**

Then the outcome:

> FieldRelay placed a real call to a vendor, got a price and an arrival time back as structured data, and raised an approval — because a price was quoted, and only a person should agree to pay it.

## What is deliberately absent

No claim about FieldRelay's own time savings. It has not been measured against a manual baseline, and inventing one would undermine everything else in the submission. **If a measured figure is wanted, run one timed manual baseline and cite it as a single observation, clearly labelled.**

## Rule

If a number cannot be traced to a source in this file, it does not go in the submission, the video, or the README.
